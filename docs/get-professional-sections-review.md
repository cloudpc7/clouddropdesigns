## At a glance

**Current content**

| Label      | Value                 |
|------------|-----------------------|
| Experience | 10+ years in IT       |
| Focus      | Web & mobile solutions |
| Role       | Founder & developer   |

**What works**

- Fast scan — three facts in one row.
- Matches how people skim a profile before reading paragraphs.
- “At a glance” is a clear, humble label (not overselling).

**What’s weak**

- **Repeats the bio and header.** “10+ years,” web/mobile, and founder/developer are already implied by your name block, title, experience line, and new bio.
- **Low information density.** After someone reads your bio, these three stats don’t add much new.
- **“Founder & developer” in a stat card** — fine once on the page; redundant as a third tile when you’re not repeating “founder” in the bio intentionally.
- **Visual weight vs. payoff.** Stat cards look like interactive UI; the content is read-only summary. That’s the same “everything in a wrapper” issue — decoration without function.

**Verdict:** **Useful for skimmers who never read the bio; redundant for everyone else.** Not bad, but the weakest *unique* value on the page.

**Options (pick one direction later)**

1. **Keep but refresh values** — swap “Founder & developer” for something the bio doesn’t say (e.g. **California-based**, **Direct founder access**, **Portfolio: Code Drops**).
2. **Merge into bio** — remove section; add one stat line under the bio if you still want scan-friendly facts.
3. **Remove** — shortest page, least repetition. Bio + What I build may be enough.

**Recommendation:** **Keep only if you change the three values** so they don’t echo the bio. Otherwise merge or cut.

## What I build

**Current content**

1. **Custom web applications** — React-based products with thoughtful UX, performance, and maintainable architecture.
2. **Mobile products** — Cross-platform builds from concept through store-ready deployment.
3. **End-to-end delivery** — Design, development, and launch — one studio, direct founder access.

**What works**

- **Strongest service section on the page.** Clear offer: web, mobile, full delivery.
- Concrete enough (**React**, **store-ready**) without jargon overload.
- Aligns with Code Drops and Drop & Elevate — this is what a prospect needs before Connect.
- Three cards is a standard, credible layout for capabilities.

**What’s weak**

- **“Direct founder access”** appears here and in the bio (“work directly with me”) and in How I work (“accessible from first conversation…”). Say it once prominently; elsewhere, imply or cut.
- **Third card overlaps card one and two** — “End-to-end delivery” is the wrapper around web + mobile; some clients will read it as repetition.
- **Panel styling on all three** — fine for separation, but all identical boxes can feel template-y (see UI note below).

**Verdict:** **Keep.** This section earns its place. It’s the bridge between *who you are* (profile) and *how to reach you* (Connect).

**Recommendations**

- Tighten card 3 so it’s not a repeat of 1+2 — e.g. emphasize **process** (scope → design → build → launch) or **ongoing support**, not “we do web and mobile again.”
- Consider one line under the section heading: *“See shipped examples in Code Drops”* — ties to portfolio without claiming revenue.
- Drop “direct founder access” from this card if the bio already carries that message.

## How I work

**Current content**

1. Understand the business problem before writing the first line of code.
2. Ship polished, scalable work — not prototypes that become permanent.
3. Stay accessible from first conversation through launch and beyond.

**What works**

- Bullet **#2 is the standouts** — specific, honest, memorable. Speaks to a real client fear (throwaway MVPs).
- Principles are short and readable.
- Fits a founder-led studio (values, not corporate process docs).

**What’s weak**

- **Heading vs. content mismatch.** “How I work” sounds like steps (discover → design → build → launch). You deliver **principles**, not a process timeline.
- **Heavy overlap with bio and other sections:**
  - #1 ≈ “understand how you want your business to show up”
  - #3 ≈ “work directly with me from first conversation through delivery”
- **Wrapped in a large panel** for three short lines — more chrome than content.
- **Generic opening** — “understand the business problem before code” is true for any good developer.

**Verdict:** **Weakest section as structured today** — not because the ideas are bad, but because the page already says most of it above. Risk: reader feels they’re re-reading the same pitch.

**Options**

1. **Rename** — “Working together,” “What you can expect,” or “Principles” (honest about content).
2. **Replace with process** — 3–4 steps: *Listen & scope → Design → Build → Launch & support* (only if you’ll keep steps short).
3. **Merge** — Fold bullet #2 into bio or What I build; delete section.
4. **Shrink** — One sentence under a smaller heading, no panel.

**Recommendation:** **Either rename + rewrite to be non-overlapping, or merge #2 into another section and remove the rest.** If you change nothing else, keep only the “no permanent prototypes” idea somewhere visible.

**Reader journey (ideal)**

2. **What I build** — what they can hire you for  
3. **One** of: short process *or* principles *or* FAQ — not all three saying similar things  
4. **Connect** (+ optional CTA to Drop & Elevate)

Right now steps 1, 2, and 3 all mention understanding the client, direct access, and delivery. That’s the main structural issue — not any single section in isolation.

## UI / “everything in a wrapper”

- **At a glance** — stat tiles: borderline; informational, not clickable. OK if content is unique; feels heavy if redundant.
- **What I build** — cards are justified; three parallel offers read clearly.
- **How I work** — one big panel for a short list is the poorest fit; plain typography would read more senior and less “dashboard.”

Aligns with your earlier cleanup (no experience pill, no profile panel): **use wrappers where they group interactive or genuinely distinct blocks; let short copy breathe.**
