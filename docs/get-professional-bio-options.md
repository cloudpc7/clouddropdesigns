## Portfolio work vs revenue (can you still use those projects?)

**Short answer:** Yes — if you describe what they **are**, not money you haven’t made yet.

Shipped work and revenue are different kinds of proof:

| You can say (true) | Don’t say (unless it’s true) |
|--------------------|------------------------------|
| “I’ve built and shipped web and mobile applications” | “I’ve generated revenue from these products” |
| “Live portfolio work includes VibeNMED and sites like Hemet Valley Tools” | “Successful businesses I’ve launched” |
| “From concept to production-ready experiences” | “Proven profitable apps” |
| “See Code Drops for examples of my design and development” | “Clients trust me because these apps make money” |

A proof point shows **you can design, build, and deploy** — not that every project is a business win. Listing real, live work is normal for a studio portfolio. You’d only cross a line if you claimed paying clients, revenue, or “successful businesses” when you mean live demos, portfolio pieces, or products still finding their market.

### Two kinds of proof (don’t mix them up)

1. **Capability proof** — “I can design, build, and ship.” Code Drops supports this.
2. **Commercial proof** — “Clients pay me / these products earn.” Add this when you have real paid client work or revenue.

Using #1 without claiming #2 is honest — not imposter syndrome.

### Proof points without revenue language

- “I’ve shipped live web and mobile work, including **VibeNMED** and business-facing sites such as **Hemet Valley Tools** and **Sonora Tires Inc**.”
- “I take ideas from concept to **production-ready** web and mobile experiences — see **Code Drops** for examples.”
- “With **10+ years in information technology**, I bring systems thinking and hands-on development to every project.”
- “As founder of CloudDropDesigns, you work **directly with me** from first conversation through launch.”

### When portfolio work belongs on Get Professional

- It shows craft, range, and that you finish things.
- Many studios show personal products, concept work, or builds that aren’t revenue engines yet.
- Prospective clients often care about **quality and fit** more than whether every app has paying users.

### When to soften wording

- Don’t name something as “shipped” if it’s unfinished or not public.
- Call practice or studio builds **portfolio work** or **Code Drops**, not “client success.”
- Use **Drop & Elevate** for paid engagement — the bio proves you *can* deliver; services/inquiry is where commercial work starts.

### Honest bio example (capability, not revenue)

> I'm Paul, founder and developer at CloudDropDesigns. I help you clarify how you want your business to communicate, then build web and mobile experiences that reflect that vision. With 10+ years in information technology, I've shipped live portfolio work including VibeNMED and business web apps such as Hemet Valley Tools — and I bring the same standards to client projects.

**Bottom line:** You’re not lying by listing real built work. Claim **shipping and skill** now; add **paid outcomes** when they’re true.
